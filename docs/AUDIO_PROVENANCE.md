# MediHome audio provenance

This file records where every sound on the MediHome website comes from, how it was made, and what is known (and not
known) about its licensing. Last updated: 24 October 2026.

## Summary

| Sound | Source | Third-party audio? |
|---|---|---|
| Background music "Night Sanctuary" (`public/audio/medihome-night-sanctuary.ogg` and `.aac`) | Original composition and synthesis in `scripts/audio/night-sanctuary.js`, rendered by `scripts/audio/build-audio.mjs` | None |
| Interaction sounds (hover/focus, click, navigation, back, close, success, error) | Original, synthesised live in the browser by `lib/sound/cues.ts` | None |

paid services were used. No audio was copied, sampled, analysed or recreated from any existing song, soundtrack,
game, console, film or artist.

## Background music: "Night Sanctuary"

- **Composition.** Written for MediHome in this project: an eight-chord progression in D-flat major (Dbmaj9, Bbm9,
  Gbmaj9, Ab add9, Fm9, Bbm11, Gbmaj7#11, Ab9sus4; 16 s each, 128 s per loop) and five short flute-like phrases
  (2 to 6 notes each) placed sparsely across the loop. The chord voicings and the phrases are set out in the source
  file. They are original and are not taken from or modelled on any existing work. Common chord types and the
  pentatonic scale are general musical vocabulary, not anyone's property.
- **Synthesis.** Sine-wave oscillators only, from the browser's standard Web Audio API:
  - **pads:** one held voice per chord note: a pure tone with two faint, nearly identical copies left and right, plus
    a little 2nd and 3rd harmonic.
  - **overtones:** two very faint high tones per chord.
  - **flute-like voice:** an additive tone of 4 harmonics with legato glides, an upward "scoop" into each phrase and
    delayed vibrato.
  - **reverb:** a convolution reverb whose impulse response is generated by the script (decaying, low-passed
    pseudo-random values with a fixed seed). This is the only place random values are used, and they only shape the
    decay of the tones.
  - There are **no noise layers** (no wind, rain, water or "air" noise).
- **The flute is synthesised.** It imitates the general behaviour of a breathy wooden flute (soft attack, glides,
  vibrato). It is not a recording of a real bamboo flute and is not presented as one.
- **Nature ambience is not included.** Wind, leaves and water cannot be synthesised convincingly without noise, and
  noise-based imitations are what made the previous version sound like static. If real nature ambience is wanted,
  the practical, lawful route is a field recording under a licence that clearly permits commercial use without
  restrictions (for example CC0), checked recording by recording and recorded in this file. None has been added.
- **Rendering and encoding** (`scripts/audio/build-audio.mjs`): the score is rendered offline at 48 kHz in a local
  Chrome browser (OfflineAudioContext), three passes back to back, keeping the middle one plus 1 s either side. That
  way every reverb tail that crosses the loop point is inside the file and the loop is seamless by construction. The
  build then normalises the peak to -3 dBFS and encodes with the browser's own encoders (WebCodecs `AudioEncoder`):
  - **Opus** (96 kb/s, written into an Ogg container by the script's own few-line muxer). Opus is royalty-free (IETF
    RFC 6716).
  - **AAC-LC** (128 kb/s, ADTS) as a fallback for browsers without Opus. AAC is a patented format. Distributing
    AAC-encoded content is common practice and its patent pool licenses devices and software rather than content,
    but this is not legal advice and has not been checked by a lawyer. The site prefers the Opus file wherever the
    browser supports it.
- **Reproducible.** `node scripts/audio/build-audio.mjs` rebuilds both files from source (needs only Node and a local
  Chrome or Edge). `--preview <folder>` also writes WAV listening previews.
- **Playback** (`components/SoundExperience.tsx`): the file is decoded once and looped with a sample-accurate
  Web Audio loop over the middle 128 s, so there is no gap or click at the loop point.

## Interface cues

MediHome's own interaction sound language, written for this project in D-flat major (the key of the music).

**What they are made of.** Every sound is built from sine tones only (some with a faint octave partial). Each has a
4-8 ms fade-in and a smooth exponential decay: no noise, no samples, no files.

**Not a copy.** They follow the general idea of console-style UI feedback: distinct, short sounds for moving, selecting
and going back. None of them copies, samples or imitates any PlayStation or other console sound. The pitches,
intervals and shapes below were chosen for MediHome.

| Sound | When | Gesture | Length* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hover | A mouse pointer enters a control (once per entry, never on touch); Tab/arrow keys move keyboard focus | Ab6 lifting a hair, with a faint Db7 | ~65 ms |
| Click | A button is clicked or activated (mouse, tap, Enter/Space) | Eb6 and Ab6 together with a soft Ab5 body: the most definite | ~110 ms |
| Navigation | Navigation links, tabs, menu items, selects and toggles changing, a menu or dialog opening | Two quick rising tones, F6 then Bb6 | ~145 ms |
| Back | Explicit in-app back links (`.back-link`); never the browser's own back button | One tone gliding down, Bb6 to Eb6 | ~115 ms |
| Close | A dialog, drawer or menu closing (✕, Esc, backdrop, Cancel) | Short glide down, Ab6 to Eb6 with a faint Db6 to Ab5 | ~75 ms |
| Success | A confirmation message appears right after the visitor acts | Rising chime, Eb6 then Ab6 | ~0.4 s |
| Error | An error message appears right after the visitor acts | Soft falling third, C6 then A5 | ~0.4 s |

\*Within 20 dB of the sound's peak.

**How they were made to stay clear over the music.** The tones sit between about 0.8 and 2.2 kHz. That is above the
music's warm register and below the piercing range. Each cue was mixed with the actual music file at the site's
default volume, at three moments (pads only, a flute phrase, a dense chord), and its tones measured against the
music in the same frequencies over its first ~40 ms. The clearest tone of every cue is at least 7.6 dB above the
music at all three moments. At default volume, click peaks around -30 dBFS, the others around -38 dBFS and hover
around -41 dBFS. The combined peak stayed below -17 dBFS, so there is no clipping. The music itself was not changed.

**Avoiding clutter.** Each action makes one sound:
- Repeats are rate-limited per category.
- A hover chime never plays on top of another sound, and a click cuts a still-ringing hover chime short (15 ms fade).
- After an action or a page change, hover chimes pause briefly, so content moving under a still pointer is silent.
- Dialog opening and closing only sounds when the click that caused it didn't already.

Interaction sounds stay silent while Ratchet speaks and when sound is off. With "reduce motion" set they default to
off. Wiring: `lib/sound/interactions.ts` (one set of document-level listeners) and `lib/sound/classify.ts` (which
control gets which sound).

## Ratchet's voice

- The greeting is spoken by the **visitor's own browser speech engine** (`window.speechSynthesis`). MediHome ships
  only the text. No voice model, voice recording or text-to-speech service is bundled, hosted or paid for, and no
  speech audio is redistributed by MediHome.
- The voice is chosen from what the visitor's device provides (`lib/sound/voice.ts`). It prefers clear adult male
  English voices, for example:
  - Microsoft Edge's "Natural" voices (Andrew, Brian, Guy)
  - "Google UK English Male" in Chrome
  - Apple's Daniel or Aaron
  - Microsoft Mark or David on Windows

  The pitch is lowered slightly and the pace slowed slightly for a composed delivery.
- **Licensing:** these voices are part of the visitor's browser or operating system and are used through the
  standard web API, as every website that uses `speechSynthesis` does. Their terms are set by the browser or OS
  vendor. MediHome does not copy or redistribute their output. Note that some voices (Google's, and Edge's "Natural"
  voices) are network voices: the browser sends the greeting text to the vendor to synthesise it. The text is fixed
  and contains no personal data.
- **Limitations:** the voice differs between devices and browsers; on a device with no male English voice a lowered
  default voice is used. The browser speaks directly to the speakers, so no audio effects (such as a subtle
  "robotic" filter) can be applied. The slightly synthetic character comes from the voices themselves. A consistent,
  processed voice for every visitor would need a pre-recorded file from a TTS engine whose licence explicitly allows
  commercial redistribution of its output. That has not been done.
- Windows' built-in SAPI voices (e.g. "Microsoft David Desktop") were **not** used to pre-record a file: their terms
  for redistributing generated audio commercially could not be verified.

## What has not been verified

- No copyright registration has been made, and no legal clearance or exclusivity is claimed. The music and cues are
  original work created for this project; their copyright status follows from that and from MediHome's agreements
  with whoever produced this code.
- The AAC patent position above is general information, not legal advice.
- Third-party browser voice terms are the browser or OS vendor's; they are not reproduced here.

## Checks made before release

- `public/audio/` contains only the two built music files and `CREDITS.md` (enforced by `tests/sound.test.tsx`).
- The previous real-time engine (`lib/sound/engine.ts`) and its noise-based layers were deleted; nothing reuses them.
- `scripts/audio/night-sanctuary.js` contains no noise source, sample, fetch or `Math.random` (enforced by tests).
- Measurements of the final render are printed by the build script and summarised in `public/audio/CREDITS.md`.
